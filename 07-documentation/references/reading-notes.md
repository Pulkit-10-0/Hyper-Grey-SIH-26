# Reading notes

Each paragraph records the equation, figure or design relationship taken from
one source and names the SeaNergy function, test or screen where it appears.
Local links point to the original PDF in `03-research`.
Function contracts are listed in [the core API](../api/core-api.md), and the
screen locations are described in [the operator guide](../user-guide/user-guide.md).

## Core design references

**`mackenzie1981`.** The nine-term sound-speed equation is transcribed term by term in `soundSpeedWater()`. Its `c(T,S,Z)` output appears on Environment and in the range relation `R = c t / 2` used for `measuredRangeM`.

**`ainslie1998`.** The simplified seawater-absorption formulation supplies the compact comparison model used to check the frequency trend of the implemented Thorp curve. The check appears beside `thorpDbPerKm()` and in notebook Figure 1.

**`francois1982a`.** The pure-water and magnesium-sulphate absorption terms establish why attenuation rises across the 100 to 500 kHz payload band. SeaNergy carries that behaviour through `absorptionDbPerM()` into `transmissionLossDb()` and `echoSnrDb()`.

**`francois1982b`.** The boric-acid and total-absorption equation supplies the environmental comparison used by the NPL calculator. SeaNergy uses the simplified `thorpDbPerKm()` path and exposes alpha in dB/km on Environment and notebook Figure 1.

**`thorp1967`.** The analytic attenuation equation is implemented directly in `thorpDbPerKm()`: two rational frequency terms, the `2.75e-4 f^2` term and `0.003`, with `f` in kHz. `absorptionDbPerM()` converts its dB/km result to dB/m.

**`urick1983`.** The monostatic active-sonar equation supplies `SL - 2TL + TS - NL + DI + PG` in `echoSnrDb()`. `decide()` applies it to every candidate and the Echo screen compares its prediction with the matched-filter measurement.

**`harris1978`.** The cosine-sum window equations and their peak-sidelobe trade-off supply `windowFunction()` and `WINDOW_PSL_DB`. The same envelope classes are generated by firmware `window_gain_q15()` and displayed on Wave and notebook Figure 3.

**`barker1953`.** The length-13 binary sequence and its aperiodic autocorrelation supply `BARKER_13` and the `barker13` branch in `synthesise()`. Peak-to-sidelobe performance appears in `matchedFilter()`, Echo and T-07.

**`azar2023`.** The paper's cognitive loop and SINR-maximising waveform selection supply the measured-environment-to-waveform structure. SeaNergy implements the bounded candidate search in `decide()` and returns echo error through `noiseCorrectionDb` in `engine.fire()`.

**`duan2023`.** The wideband ambiguity-function and multi-parameter waveform result supplies the bandwidth/resolution objective. SeaNergy ranks feasible candidates by bandwidth in `decide()` and converts bandwidth to `c / (2B)` in `rangeResolutionChirp()`.

**`nplAbsorption`.** The NPL calculator's Francois-Garrison, Fisher-Simmons and Ainslie-McColm comparison curves supply the external absorption cross-check. Notebook Figure 1 and the asserts around `thorpDbPerKm()` use the same dB/km quantity.

**`domingos2022`.** The survey's taxonomy of receive-side classification and time-frequency inputs supplies the boundary between recognition and transmit adaptation. Notebook Sections 8 to 12 keep the physics solver as guard and learn only `error_db`; the runtime analogue is `noiseCorrectionDb`.

**`pib2021`.** The Deep Ocean Mission programme components supply the AUV/deep-sea deployment context for the system architecture and Mission screen. No acoustic coefficient is taken from the release; it locates the payload within the MoES problem context.
