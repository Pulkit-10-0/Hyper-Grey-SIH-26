01 · Problem and mechanism

Why a fixed waveform is the wrong answer

Acoustic absorption in sea water rises steeply with frequency. A 350 kHz pulse resolves millimetres and dies inside a few hundred metres; a 100 kHz pulse travels far and resolves centimetres. Suspended sediment makes the trade-off worse and does so unpredictably — an estuary plume can move a survey line from clear water to 800 NTU in minutes.

A payload that picks one frequency before launch is therefore optimised for conditions it will not meet for most of the mission. It is either wasting resolution or losing the seabed.

The same solver, the same sonar equation and the same compression code run in both. Only the medium constants change. That is the point of building it as software rather than as a tuned circuit.

Where the excess loss comes from

Suspended sediment adds attenuation on top of the medium's own absorption, and it rises with frequency faster than absorption does. The model is referenced to each medium's own band so a single coefficient means the same thing in air and in water: 

excessScatteringDbPerM(cfg, ntu, f)
  = cfg.scatterCoeff × ntu × (f / cfg.scatterRefHz)^1.5

  water   scatterRefHz 223 kHz    scatterCoeff 9.2e-5
  air     scatterRefHz  38.2 kHz  scatterCoeff 1.51e-3

At the reference frequency and 800 NTU the scattering term roughly matches the medium's own absorption. That calibration is an engineering choice, and it is flagged as one in the reality ledger.

03 · Waveform mathematics

Resolution without energy

An unmodulated pulse has to choose: short enough to resolve, or long enough to be heard. Sweeping the frequency across the pulse breaks that link. Range resolution stops depending on pulse length and starts depending on bandwidth. 

unmodulated    Δr = cτ/2          resolution costs energy
chirped        Δr = c/2B          resolution costs bandwidth
gain           G = 10 log₁₀(τB)      the pulse is recovered by correlation
Pulse τ (ms)Unmodulated Δr (m) Chirped Δr (mm)Time–bandwidth Compression gain (dB)

A 10 ms pulse that resolves 7.7 m unmodulated resolves 2.6 mm chirped across 299 kHz, and arrives 34.8 dB stronger after correlation — at identical transmitted energy. Everything else on this page is in service of that line.

Transmit window

The envelope is tapered before it reaches the driver. That protects the output stage from a hard edge and it sets the sidelobe floor of the compressed echo, which is what decides whether a small target next to a large one is visible at all.

Barker-13 keeps a constant envelope, which suits a driver running at its limit. No longer binary Barker sequence is known to exist, so 13 is the practical ceiling rather than an arbitrary choice.

04 · Firmware architecture

The processor is asleep while the pulse goes out

The naive way to emit a chirp is to evaluate sinf() per sample. At sonar sample rates that keeps the CPU pinned for the whole pulse and, above a few hundred kilosamples per second, it cannot keep up at all — the waveform has to be precomputed into a buffer with the core awake throughout.

SeaNergy uses a phase accumulator against a quarter-wave lookup table. Each sample is an add, a shift and an index. The samples are handed to DMA and the core drops back to idle. 

phase += phaseStep;                 // 32-bit accumulator, wraps for free
sample = TABLE[phase >> 22];        // 1024-entry quarter wave
phaseStep += chirpRate;             // linear sweep, no transcendental

Two of those rejections are the ones that usually go unnoticed until a bench session fails. A JSN-SR04T is sold as a waterproof ultrasonic sensor; it is waterproof, not submersible, and it will not range through water.

Transducer

A piezo transducer is a capacitor with a sharp mechanical resonance, not a resistive load. The drive stage sees clamped capacitance off resonance and a real impedance minimum at it, so a series matching inductor is what decides how much of the drive energy becomes sound. The impedance sweep and the matching calculation live in 01-hardware/bench-setup.

Duty cycle is what makes the budget work. The payload sleeps between pings, wakes for roughly 12 ms, senses for a 50 ms window, transmits for milliseconds, and returns to sleep. The transmit bar dominates the ping, which is correct — that is the energy that leaves as sound. The saving is taken out of everything that is not sound.

Read the middle of that table carefully. Between 180 and 320 NTU the solver does not drop frequency — it lengthens the pulse from 0.5 ms to 2.8 ms, buying 7.5 dB of compression gain and holding resolution at 2.6 mm. Only when that runs out does it give up bandwidth. That ordering was not programmed; it falls out of scoring the candidates.
