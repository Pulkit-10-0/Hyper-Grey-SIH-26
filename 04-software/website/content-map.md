## The argument, in order

1. Frequency buys resolution and costs range. **(01)**
2. Sediment moves that trade-off, unpredictably, mid-mission. **(01)**
3. So the pulse has to be chosen at runtime, not before launch. **(01, 02)**
4. Chirping breaks the resolution/energy link, which is what makes runtime
   choice worth having. **(03)**
5. It can be synthesised without keeping the processor awake. **(04, 06)**
6. On parts that exist, with the failures documented. **(05)**
7. And it demonstrably does the right thing across the sediment range. **(07)**
8. Here is the working artefact. **(08)**
9. Here is exactly what we have and have not proven. **(09)**
10. Here is what it rests on. **(10)**

Sections 01 through 08 are the case. Section 09 is what makes it credible.

Once tank data exists, the measured series go into `06-validation/tank-results`
and are added to the sweep charts as a second line. The reality ledger row for
tank results changes from planned to measured at the same time, in the same
commit. Those two changes travel together or neither happens.
